# Email orders justify a narrower build

Research checked 28 October 2026. Desk research only; no businesses were contacted.

**Recommendation: modify the project and proceed as a portfolio prototype. Commercial demand for our version remains unproven.** The user's objection is correct for website orders that already reach the supplier's order system cleanly. However, verified UK supplier pages show that website checkout and emailed purchase orders coexist. A customer can know the exact product and still send a document that needs transferring into the supplier's system. That makes order capture and validation a credible problem, while AI searching for vaguely described products should be secondary. Existing vendors already sell this workflow, so we should not present Pennbridge as a new, unserved market.

**A website does not necessarily replace emailed purchase orders.** Production Automation, a UK controls and automation supplier, advertises website purchases and separately allows trade-account customers to email purchase orders within their credit limit. Mistral Industrial Chemicals gives an especially clear example: choose products on its website, prepare a company purchase order with product names, sizes and quantities, then email it. These are two advertised buying routes, not evidence that customers cannot identify the products. ([Production Automation](https://www.production-automation.co.uk/pages/contact-us), [Mistral](https://mistralni.co.uk/pages/purchase-orders))

Workplace Gloves similarly offers checkout with a PO reference, emailed requirements and document upload. Gloves N Stuff accepts both online checkout and emailed official POs, including PDF and Word files. These examples confirm that the channel exists across several supplier categories. They do **not** tell us the email share, manual-entry workload, existing automation or willingness to pay at those firms. None should be labelled a qualified sales prospect on this evidence alone. ([Workplace Gloves](https://workplacegloves.co.uk/purchase-orders-b2b/), [Gloves N Stuff](https://www.glovesnstuff.com/pages/purchase-orders))

**The practical gap is between two business systems.** NMBS OnePlace describes merchants emailing or uploading PDF/XML purchase orders generated by their back-office systems. It maps PDFs, validates details such as active product codes and minimum order value, and sends qualifying orders to suppliers. This directly documents the situation our demo should represent: a buyer has already specified an order, but the order must reach another company's system. It also demonstrates an existing alternative to building custom software. ([OnePlace](https://www.oneplace.co.uk/order-mapping/))

The resulting fictional demonstration should be simple: a customer chooses known parts, generates a PDF PO in its purchasing software and emails it. Pennbridge creates a draft sales order, checks codes, quantities, units and prices, highlights one discrepancy, and imports the approved result. The value is reduced rekeying and reliable checks. A message saying “same bearings as last time” remains a useful difficult case, but it should not define the ordinary workflow.

Email itself does not establish a need for AI. RS's Spanish eOrdering page describes receiving structured XML through several channels, including email, and integrating it into its ERP. If the information already arrives in a supported structured format, we should parse and validate it directly. The same principle applies to integrated website orders. ([RS eOrdering](https://es.rs-online.com/web/content/servicios/soluciones-compras/eordering))

**Established products already cover much of our feature list.** Conexiom advertises emailed PO intake, checks against ERP product/pricing/shipping data and exception review. Esker advertises order extraction, validation, review routing and ERP posting across email and structured channels. “AI reads purchase orders” and “humans review exceptions” therefore are not distinctive commercial claims. No comparable all-in vendor prices were obtained, so there is no basis to say established tools are too expensive for small firms or that our custom build would be cheaper. ([Conexiom](https://info.conexiom.com/manufacturing), [Conexiom FAQ](https://conexiom.com/faq/), [Esker](https://www.esker.com/solutions/order-management/))

There is also named industrial adoption: Esker's April 2022 case story describes Routeco, a UK industrial automation distributor, using its Order Management solution. That supports the operational relevance of this software category. It remains a historical vendor-published case study, not independent evidence of current performance or demand at a small business. We should not borrow its claimed savings for Pennbridge. ([Esker Routeco case](https://www.esker.com/en-gb/blog/customer-stories-order-cash/orders-orders-orders-happy-days/))

**The build should change in five ways.** First, implement exact SKU and maintained customer-alias matching before fuzzy catalogue search. Second, add genuine machine-generated PDF fixtures early; the current 50 email cases contain text/HTML attachments, so they do not yet test the central document format. Third, choose one concrete destination import schema and verify an approved draft actually enters a test order system. A generic CSV download proves only an intermediate step. Fourth, add a structured-input route that skips AI. Fifth, keep product, unit and price information sourced from the destination system rather than creating a second independently maintained catalogue.

The 10,000-SKU catalogue remains useful for testing indexed search and near-identical variants. It is not evidence of business demand. Likewise, the 50 authored cases are an exception-heavy acceptance suite, not a representative order sample. Their mix cannot establish a real business's exception rate, labour savings or return on investment. We need a separate ordinary-workload evaluation and must count review, corrections, failed imports and manual exceptions in the time measurement.

**Before proposing a paid implementation, validate the gap with a real operator.** Ask an order-desk employee to show the last emailed order from arrival to recorded sales order. Record what they typed, checked and corrected; establish how many orders follow that path; and inspect their existing import, portal and automation options. Request permissioned, anonymised examples and a named person willing to assess a shadow-mode trial. These are proposed discovery steps; no interviews or sample access have occurred.

Our proposed commercial gate is at least one business with demonstrated recurring rekeying, usable master data, a feasible destination integration and a process owner prepared to test. Measure manual and assisted work on the same representative inputs, compare the total cost with existing options, and agree what improvement would justify proceeding. Stop or change direction if the channel is already automated, its volume is negligible, review eliminates the time saving, or an existing tool solves the problem more effectively.

For the CV, this remains a defensible project: a measured document-to-order prototype, with explicit limits and a clear explanation of when AI is unnecessary. The strongest commercial next step is observing a real workflow, not adding more features to the fictional company.
